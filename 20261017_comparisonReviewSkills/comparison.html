Review skills compared

Built-in /code-review (Claude Code 2.1.302, default level = high) vs own /reviewBranch · 2 repositories, 4 branches, 16 runs · 17–18 Oct 2026

Verdict. By the rule fixed before the runs, /reviewBranch stays the default: cost is a tie and /code-review fails the 50% precision gate (44% vs 91%; 5 false findings vs 0). But the rule does not weigh missed bugs. Where the owner's own later fixes give a ground truth, /code-review found the high-severity regression in 2 of 2 runs and a safety-gate bug in 4 of 4. /reviewBranch found them in 1 of 2 and 0 of 4. Use both on safety-relevant code and before a release. Teach /reviewBranch the removed-behaviour check.

5 · Good and bad, short

/reviewBranch

Good
  • 91% of its findings are real; 0 false.
  • Severity matches the owner's when it finds the defect.
  • Correct base branch, stated up front; gives a merge verdict.
  • Stable and owned.
Bad
  • Misses defects: 0/4 on the safety-gate bug, 1/2 on the high one.
  • Its "mergeable" verdict was wrong in 3 of 4 hr runs.
  • Large variance between two runs of the same branch.
  • Ignores uncommitted work; fills the main context.

/code-review

Good
  • Found every owner-known defect that either skill found, in every run.
  • Systematic angles, including removed behaviour and conventions.
  • Fix, PR comments, ultra; isolated context.
  • Same cost as /reviewBranch.
Bad
  • 56% of its list is weak or false: the triage work moves to the reader.
  • No verify pass at the default level; 5 false findings.
  • No severity, no verdict; weak base-branch logic for master repos.
  • Behaviour changes with Claude Code releases.

6 · Limits of this test

  • Two repositories, four branches, n = 2. Two runs show the spread, not a distribution.
  • The ground truth came from a later AI-assisted review by the owner, so it is not independent.
  • Only the default level of /code-review (high) was measured.
  • Two hr runs were killed by the usage limit. They were quarantined and re-run about 5 h later.
  • Real vs weak is a judgement. Each call is written down with its evidence; on AlliumAssault, three calls move /code-review's precision by about ±5 points.
