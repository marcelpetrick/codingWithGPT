Überblick und Zusammenfassungen:
There is often a deep divide within companies that develop embedded cyber-physical systems, such as cars or medical devices:

On the one hand, traditional hardware engineering with its slow development cycles still shapes the organization’s processes. Here, linear and sequential thinking is deeply ingrained in the culture: “getting it right the first time” leads to big upfront design work. In safety-critical domains such as automotive or medical, this is often reinforced by regulatory standards that are structured around phase-gate processes. As a result, products are delivered late and, due to a lack of feedback, often do not meet market needs.

On the other hand, there is a growing need for rapid response, whether to new market requirements or disrupted supply chains. Moreover, developing innovative products requires experimentation and rapid feedback to master their complexity. Agility addresses these needs, but attempts to introduce agile approaches in hardware-focused organizations often end in pseudo-agility in a waterfall corset due to the deeply entrenched processes.

Realizing the potential of agility requires a fundamental shift in both development processes and architecture. In our talk, we will share specific practices from our experience, such as:

• Extending the notion of “flexible architecture” from software development to systems and hardware development to enable experimentation and adaptability
• Breaking up hardware-software silos to enable early and continuous hardware-software integration
• Introducing agile development practices in hardware development
• Establishing a system model to bridge the gap between software and hardware developers and improve their collaboration
• Using open-source tools that can be automated and easily integrated

We will show how these architectural and organizational changes enable a more responsive, truly agile systems engineering process.

Art der Vermittlung:
The talk presents the methods and approaches using a project example.

Nutzen:
The talk will present field-tested practices for agile systems development using a comprehensive, practical case study.
